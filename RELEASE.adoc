= officework 0.5.0-beta.5

日本語版: link:RELEASE.ja.adoc[RELEASE.ja.adoc]

To try it: `pip install --pre officework`

Two published government forms taught us this release. We converted the
National Tax Agency's liquor tax overview and the Cabinet Office's assessment
notice, and compared each against the PDF the same office publishes beside it.
Everything below came out of that comparison.

== Spreadsheets print the way the file asks

Seven defects, all of them visible on the first page of that tax form.

**Text in a cell did not wrap.** The header cell holds 販 売 場 数 over Number
of licensed sites to sell liquors, with wrapping set. We ran it out as one
line: through its neighbours, past the right margin, and off the paper. It
wraps now, and to the width the cell really spans, which matters because these
headers are merged across columns.

**Formatting inside a cell was ignored.** That same cell is 8pt with its
English line at 6pt in a different family. The whole cell printed at one size,
which also moved every wrap with it. Each run is drawn at its own size now.

**Only one font could be embedded in a PDF.** The form asks for ＭＳ明朝 in the
body, ＭＳゴシック in the headings and Century for the English; we printed all
of it in one face. As many as are needed are embedded, and a character its font
does not have falls back rather than disappearing.

**Negative numbers ignored the format's negative section.** Number formats come
in sections — positive, negative, zero, text — and this form asks for
「△ 5,148」. We read the first section and put a minus in front.

**Distributed alignment printed left**, where the 区分 column should spread its
characters across the cell, and **centred cells printed left** as well, since
only left and right were distinguished.

**A sheet's vertical margins were taken from its horizontal ones.** This form
sets 25mm at the sides and 30mm top and bottom. Page one was right; every page
after it began 5mm too high with 5mm too much room at the foot, which is two
extra lines a page.

== Documents keep their indents, their text boxes and their rules

**Indentation was being lost.** Japanese forms indent with spaces rather than
with a paragraph setting, and three separate things dropped it: the left indent
was rounded to steps of two full-width characters, so a one-character indent
became two; indents written as a character count were not read at all, which is
what Japanese Word writes; and full-width spaces typed at the start of a line
were discarded, or drawn at the width of an ASCII space. On the Cabinet Office
notice this put the addressee 76.7pt from where it belongs. It now sits at
178.90pt against the reference PDF's 178.92.

**Text boxes made by other programs never reached the page.** They survived
being opened and saved — they were kept as the original XML — but only shapes
we had written ourselves were drawn. The three contact panels on page 6 of that
notice, and all five lines inside each, are printed now.

**Table borders were not read.** Every table came out with all four sides
because the model had nowhere to put what the file said. A form of underlined
entry fields came out as a grid of boxes. What the file asks for is drawn, per
table and per cell, and a docx table that says nothing gets nothing, which is
what Word draws.

**Parts were being found by their conventional names.** `word/styles.xml` and
its neighbours are a convention, not a rule; OOXML says where its parts are
through the relationships. One of these forms uses `word/styles2.xml`, so we
were reading no styles, no settings and no theme at all — 403 style
declarations, invisible.

== Converting a sheet to AsciiDoc

Cell rich text stays out of adoc, so what has to survive is the shape of the
text. Three things did not.

A line break inside a cell became a blank line, which reads as two paragraphs;
it is written as AsciiDoc's own line break now. Leading and trailing whitespace
was trimmed, taking the indents with it; AsciiDoc's own mark for an empty
span now protects it. And
cells flow into rows by column count, but the cut let a cell in without
checking that it fitted, so a wide merged cell overflowed its row and
everything after it slid down and across.

That tax sheet now round-trips all 557 of its cells unchanged. Fifty-six of
them changed before.

== Other changes

* The right panel is a tree, like an IDE's, and opening a file reveals it there
* Ribbon buttons that do one thing are grouped into one row

== Under the hood

The PDF writer's font handling was built around a single embedded font — one
face, one subset, one resource. It now keeps a set, which is what made the
per-cell fonts possible. The single-font entry points are unchanged.

`Paper` carries four margins instead of one. Adding the fields made the
compiler name every construction site, twenty-five of them, so none could be
missed.
